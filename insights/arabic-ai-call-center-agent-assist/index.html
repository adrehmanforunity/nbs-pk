AI in Arabic Call Centres: Start with Agent Assistance, Quality Review and Better Handoffs

AI can support service work in focused ways, but useful deployment starts with a well-defined task, tested language performance, and employees who remain able to review and correct outputs.

By National Business Services · Published 26 October 2026

Contact-centre teams may consider AI for conversation summaries, knowledge retrieval, agent prompts, transcription, or quality review. These applications have different risks and data needs. They should be evaluated as separate workflows rather than bundled into a broad promise of “AI-powered service.”

Start with assistance before autonomous action

An agent-assistance tool can present an approved knowledge article or draft a summary while an employee remains responsible for the customer interaction. This may be a more controllable starting point than allowing a system to make account changes or resolve every request without review.

Choose one repetitive, bounded task. Define what information the AI can use, what it may suggest, what an employee must verify, and what the system is not allowed to do. Keep the agent able to ignore or correct a suggestion without creating friction.

Check Arabic language quality for the actual task

Transcribing a call, summarizing it, and recommending a response are distinct tasks. Test the language varieties, accents, code-switching, audio conditions, and service terminology your operation actually handles. An error in a summary can change the meaning of a complaint; an incorrect prompt can steer an agent toward an unsuitable answer.

  • Use representative examples reviewed by qualified Arabic speakers.
  • Check names, numbers, negation, commitments, and sensitive details explicitly.
  • Review performance by task and language variety instead of relying on one average score.
  • Provide a correction route and record recurring error patterns.

Design quality review as decision support

Automated analysis may help teams identify calls for human review or organize quality signals, but a model-generated label is not automatically a fair or accurate assessment of an employee. Explain what is measured, validate it against human review, and give employees a process to challenge or contextualize a result.

Do not use sentiment or emotion inference as a proxy for customer intent or employee performance without a clear justification and appropriate review. Consider language, context, recording quality, and the consequences of a mistaken label.

Protect context and give customers a path to a person

When AI summarizes or assists during a conversation, show employees the relevant source information where possible. Make it clear when a suggestion is uncertain, and require a person to verify high-impact details before acting. For customer-facing automation, define how a person can take over and what context is transferred.

Consider access to recordings and transcripts, retention periods, approved processing locations, and the use of data to improve third-party models. These details depend on vendor terms, configuration, organizational controls, and applicable local requirements.

Measure outcomes, not just automation volume

  • Accuracy of summaries and usefulness of knowledge suggestions.
  • Resolution quality, repeat contact, and escalation outcomes.
  • Time agents spend reviewing or correcting AI output.
  • Quality-review agreement between automated flags and trained human reviewers.
  • Customer and employee feedback, including errors and complaints.

Set thresholds before a pilot and review both intended benefits and new burdens. If employees spend more time checking incorrect suggestions, the workflow may need redesign even if it generates many summaries.

Run a controlled pilot

Use a limited team and a defined interaction type. Train agents on the tool’s limits, establish incident reporting, sample outputs for review, and compare results against the current process. Reassess after changes to models, prompts, data sources, or workflows.

NIST’s Generative AI Profile offers a voluntary general framework for identifying and managing generative-AI risks; it is not a local regulatory requirement or a deployment certification. NBS describes OmniCX AI assistance as configuration-dependent; confirm the exact functions, Arabic language support, and data handling before selecting a use case.
