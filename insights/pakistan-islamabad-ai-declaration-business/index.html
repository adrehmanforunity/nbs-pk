Pakistan’s Islamabad AI Declaration: what businesses should know

The Ministry of IT & Telecommunication’s Islamabad AI Declaration sets out principles for Pakistan’s approach to artificial intelligence. For businesses, it is useful context—but it should not be mistaken for a complete implementation plan or a new set of company-specific legal requirements.

By National Business Services · Published 7 October 2026 · Declaration dated 9 February 2026

Pakistan’s Ministry of IT & Telecommunication published the Islamabad AI Declaration, dated 9 February 2026. It describes a sovereign and inclusive approach to AI, led by the private sector and enabled by government, intended to advance digital society, the digital economy, and digital governance. The declaration emphasizes public trust, measurable public outcomes, national capability, safeguards, and implementation.

This article summarizes the declaration’s stated direction and offers general questions for corporate discussion. It does not claim that a particular government program, funding offer, regulator, or company obligation has been created by the declaration.

What the declaration says

The Ministry’s declaration presents nine principles. In summary, these address:

  • Using AI to support national productivity and public value.
  • Keeping human accountability and lawful authority in decisions with public consequences.
  • Starting with practical use cases and looking for demonstrable impact before scaling.
  • Governing data with attention to Pakistan’s laws, privacy, security, and public trust.
  • Making government AI systems proportionate to risk, explainable, and auditable, especially where public rights or critical state functions are affected.
  • Coordinating governance across government institutions.
  • Building capability through education, research, talent, inclusion, and responsible innovation.
  • Enabling private-sector participation while developing compute capacity and digital infrastructure.
  • Engaging internationally on AI standards and governance while maintaining national interests.

These are the declaration’s stated principles, summarized here in our own words. The declaration also says its approach aims to be private-sector-led and government-enabled.

What it could mean for Pakistani businesses

The declaration is a signal of policy direction and a useful prompt for organizations evaluating AI. Its themes overlap with sound business planning: identify a real problem, measure outcomes, protect information, maintain appropriate oversight, and build skills before expanding a pilot.

For corporate leaders, the practical implication is to follow relevant government updates and assess AI use cases thoughtfully—not to assume that the declaration itself approves a product, provides funding, or automatically imposes a new compliance process on every company.

Questions leaders can use in an internal review

  • What customer, operational, or organizational problem are we trying to solve?
  • What measurable result would justify a pilot, and what evidence would we need before scaling it?
  • What data would the system use, where is it handled, and who is accountable for access and protection?
  • Which decisions require human review, an escalation path, or a documented rationale?
  • What skills, infrastructure, supplier checks, and ongoing support would be needed?
  • Which current laws, regulator guidance, contractual commitments, and sector rules apply to our specific use case?

The answers will vary by sector, data, deployment model, and intended use. Businesses should check applicable requirements with their own qualified advisers and monitor official government publications for further measures or implementation details.

Declaration versus implementation

A declaration of principles communicates direction and priorities. By itself, it does not establish the details of an operational program, a funding application, a regulator’s procedure, or a binding rule for every private organization. Those details, if introduced, need to be verified in the relevant official policy, law, notification, program documentation, or regulator guidance.

The Ministry page states Pakistan’s approach and principles; it should not be read as confirmation that all of the proposed capabilities, governance arrangements, or outcomes are already implemented.

Primary source

Islamabad AI Declaration — Ministry of IT & Telecommunication, Government of Pakistan (dated 9 February 2026).
